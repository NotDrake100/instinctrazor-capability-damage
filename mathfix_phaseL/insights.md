# sleep-shield: research insights (compiled 2026-10-17, IST)

Idea: a nightly LoRA "sleep" fine-tune that freezes skill-critical layers, so the model learns new facts without losing skills.

**How sources were checked.** [F] means I fetched the page and read it. [S] means I only saw it in search-result snippets and did not fetch it, so treat it as unverified. [X] means a public X post returned by the X search API (the post text was read, the linked papers were not). Numbers below are quoted only from [F] sources.

## Closest prior work (scoop risk)
1. **PALoRA: Projection-Adaptive LoRA for Preserving Reasoning in LLMs** [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24549 . **Highest overlap.** It has the same goal: inject facts with LoRA while protecting "skill-critical" structure in MLP weights. Step 1 trains an SVF expert on a skill and uses it to find skill-critical singular directions. Step 2 trains LoRA on unknown TriviaQA facts with an orthogonality penalty against those directions. Setup: Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B; skills GSM8K, MBPP and ARC; 100–1000 facts; baselines LoRA, OPLoRA and CorDA. Reported result: about 95% of the SVF expert's reasoning is kept. It also includes a random-k vs top-k subspace ablation. The difference from sleep-shield: PALoRA protects at the subspace level, not the layer level, it runs a single injection rather than nightly/sequential updates, and it has no sleep framing. Its own limitations section lists "continual adaptation settings" as future work, which is exactly sleep-shield's opening.
2. **Language Models Need Sleep: Learning to Self-Modify and Consolidate Memories** (Behrouz, Hashemi, Javanmard, Mirrokni; Google/Cornell) [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03979 . This paper owns the "LLM sleep" framing. Its sleep phase has two stages: (1) memory consolidation, which expands parameters with new low-rank experts and distills into them while the rest of the model stays frozen ("Knowledge Seeding"); (2) "Dreaming", RL-generated synthetic rehearsal built on SEAL. It evaluates on SQuAD knowledge incorporation, ARC few-shot, class-incremental learning, and AIME/HMMT. The paper notes that a version has been public on OpenReview since Sep 2025. sleep-shield has to cite it and position against it. The distinction: no Nested-Learning/Hope architecture is required, a standard pretrained LLM is used, and frozen layers are selected by measured importance.
3. **Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning** (Lin et al., FAIR/Meta) [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103 . The strongest "learn facts, don't forget" result to date. When learning a stream of TriviaQA facts, NaturalQuestions F1 drops 89% with full fine-tuning, 71% with LoRA, and 11% with sparse memory fine-tuning. It needs a memory-layer architecture. This is the benchmark protocol and the number to beat or match.
4. **Where to Adapt Matters: Layer-Selective Fine-Tuning for Capability Retention (LS-LoRA)** [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11620 . Puts LoRA only on layers with low input–output cosine similarity, a forward-only proxy shown to track Fisher scores (Spearman −0.76 to −0.91). It beats all-layer LoRA, L2-SP, EWC, **and a random-layer baseline** on Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B. The target tasks are math and code, not facts. This is prior art on the layer-selection mechanism itself: sleep-shield's novelty cannot be "select layers to freeze".
5. Other related work, search snippets only [S]: LayRA (LoRA on first and last layers only, to add languages) https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11414 ; HFT, Half Fine-Tuning https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.626.pdf ; DOC orthogonal continual fine-tuning https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23893 ; sleep-replay consolidation after sequential tasks https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08447 ; LoRAC-IPC https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13407 . Also an [X] post describing "Local Support Learning", gated adapters against forgetting: https://x.com/yesnoerror/status/2106489912170086572 . I did not read the underlying paper.

## Foundations (fetched)
- **LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less** [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673 . Setup: Llama-2-7B on code/math, CPT and IFT. Findings: LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning, and rank works as the learn/forget knob. Full-FT weight perturbations have rank 10–100× higher than typical LoRA configurations. MLP modules are "the primary loci" of LoRA gains. The paper recommends alpha=2r and an LR sweep. Forgetting was measured only on HellaSwag, WinoGrande and ARC-C.
- **ROME** [F] https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262 . Causal tracing finds factual recall concentrated in mid-layer MLPs at the last subject token (around layer 15–18 in GPT-2 XL). The CounterFact benchmark scores efficacy, paraphrase generalization, neighborhood specificity and fluency. ROME's own limitation section points to MEMIT (arXiv 2210.07229, cited, not fetched) for mass editing.

## (1) Insights to make the result bigger and more memorable
1. **Sharpen the claim to a sequential/nightly one.** Single-shot injection with skill protection is taken (PALoRA). The open claim is "after N nights (e.g. 30–100) of fact updates, skills stay flat while all-layer LoRA and replay degrade." Plot skill accuracy vs night index. A cumulative-drift curve is memorable, and none of the fetched works shows it for a standard LLM.
2. **Exploit an apparent tension between skill localization and fact localization.** ROME puts facts in mid-layer MLPs. LS-LoRA finds high-Fisher (target-sensitive) layers early and late, with the shape varying by model (my reading of their Fig. 2). The test: does freezing skill-critical layers leave the mid-layer MLPs free for facts? Show a layer map with fact-causal-trace importance and skill-Fisher importance overlaid. If the two are separable, that gives a clean mechanistic story and figure.
3. **Use strong, matched baselines.** All-layer LoRA, full FT, LoRA+replay (generic data and/or self-generated), random-layer freezing with the same trainable-param count, LS-LoRA, PALoRA/OPLoRA, MEMIT for facts, sparse memory FT if feasible, and RAG as the "why not just retrieve" baseline.
4. **Design the evals to separate memorization from knowledge.** Use the CounterFact-style axes: efficacy, paraphrase, neighborhood specificity, fluency. Add multi-hop and implication questions about the new facts, and probes on whether the old fact was overwritten vs coexists. Use post-cutoff real facts (dated news) as well as synthetic or fictional entities to rule out contamination.
5. **Demo and scaling angles.** A "model that reads the news every night for a month" demo with a daily dashboard: new-fact QA rising, GSM8K/HumanEval/IFEval flat. Scale across model size (1B→8B+), facts per night, and number of nights. Test whether the set of critical layers is stable across nights or drifts. Recomputing it nightly could itself be part of the "sleep" step.
6. Optional "sleep" ingredients that make the name more than a metaphor: replaying self-generated samples of old skills during the nightly run (cheap, no stored data), and a consolidation step that merges each night's adapter into a long-term one. Note that Behrouz et al. already use expansion, distillation and dreaming, so differentiate.

## (2) Pitfalls reviewers will attack
1. **Prior art and novelty.** PALoRA (skill-protected fact injection), LS-LoRA (layer selection plus a random-layer ablation), Behrouz et al. ("sleep" for LLMs), HFT/LayRA (partial freezing). The contribution has to be stated narrowly: nightly sequential updates on a standard LLM plus a layer-level skill/fact separation analysis.
2. **Missing baselines.** Replay (especially self-generated replay), random-layer freezing at matched parameter count, plain all-layer LoRA with a tuned rank and LR (Biderman et al. show LoRA is very sensitive to these), EWC/L2-SP, MEMIT, and RAG.
3. **Compute and capacity fairness.** Freezing layers reduces trainable parameters, and fewer parameters alone reduce forgetting ("learns less, forgets less"). Report Pareto curves (learning vs forgetting) across rank, LR and steps, the way Sparse Memory FT and Biderman et al. do, not single points. Count the cost of the layer-importance probe too.
4. **Measuring fact retention vs generalization.** Exact-match recall on the training phrasing is not knowledge. Require paraphrase, neighborhood/specificity, multi-hop, and retention of earlier nights' facts (backward transfer within the new-fact stream).
5. **Contamination and the definition of "new".** Facts must be verified as unknown to the base model beforehand (PALoRA uses an Unknown vs HighlyKnown labeling, following Pletenev et al.). Skill benchmarks such as GSM8K or HumanEval may be contaminated; add held-out or recent skill evals.
6. **Narrow forgetting metrics.** HellaSwag, ARC and WinoGrande are coarse. Include instruction-following, safety/refusal behavior, calibration, long-context, and generation diversity (Biderman et al. report LoRA preserves diversity better than full FT).
7. **The skill/fact disentanglement assumption may fail.** PALoRA argues skill information is spread across the singular spectrum, not localized at top components. Layer-level freezing could be too coarse. Show per-skill critical-layer maps and the overlap between skills.
8. **Seeds and variance.** Small benchmarks (MBPP, HumanEval) need multiple seeds and confidence intervals.
